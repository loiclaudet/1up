## Design Context

from lodz and his friend, 2026-10-13. lodz's direction: beautiful, not a clone of rewasd; the pads drawn as the
real ones look, and nothing else nintendo-like in the interface, so the project is safe as open source.

### Users

players on windows who own nintendo pads: the switch online nes and famicom pads first (lodz's friend, who plays
emulators and pays for rewasd today), then the switch pro, the joy-cons, the snes pad. they open 1up at their desk
to get a pad recognized, check every button answers, and bend the mapping when a game expects otherwise; then it
sits in the tray while they play. in the friend's words: "simple et efficace", and "reconfigurer les touches avec
leur visuel".

### Brand Personality

calm, precise, crafted. a small tool made with care: it shows the pad exactly as it is, says what each control
does in plain words, and stays out of the way. confidence that the pad works; a small spark when a control lights
as it is pressed.

### Aesthetic Direction

- the hero is the pad, drawn faithfully (shape, colours, button layout of the real one) by 1up itself: never
  nintendo's own images, never a logo. it sits on a light studio stage, like a product on a table
- the mapping is written on the picture: each control has a callout, a thin leader line to a label saying what it
  sends, like an annotated technical drawing. a press lights the control and its callout
- light, quiet surfaces: an off-white page with a soft stage, warm near-black ink. one live colour, a highlighter
  lime, for what is pressed or live, like an led. no nintendo red, no switch-like tiles, no xbox green, no
  mushrooms or game characters
- type: Darker Grotesque for the display (the wordmark, a pad's name), Onest for the interface. bundled, so the
  app works offline
- anti-references: rewasd's dense dark panels, gamer dashboards (dark with neon, glows), the switch's own menus,
  generic settings screens (cards in grids, toggles everywhere)

### Design Principles

1. the pad is the interface: you see it, press it, click it; lists come second
2. say it on the picture: a control's mapping is written beside it, not in a table elsewhere
3. live feedback over status text: a press lights the control and what it sends
4. a pad works with no setup: the defaults are right for most games (by position), changes are optional
5. quiet chrome, loud pad: neutral paper and ink around it, colour only where it means something
